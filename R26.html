Redmond Plumbing Tips and Guides: Our Homeowner Library

Most plumbing companies want you to know nothing — it makes the invoice easier. We take the opposite view: an informed homeowner catches problems early, asks better questions, and never overpays. Our Redmond plumbing tips and guides library is where we put everything we have learned on the job. Here is a tour.

Start Here: The Guides Everyone Reads

New to the library? Begin with the greatest hits. Our symptom-to-cause walkthrough helps you match drips, smells, gurgles, and bangs to their likely sources before you pick up the phone. The leak-documentation guide shows you exactly what to photograph and note when you find water where it should not be — the same notes that make insurance claims and repair quotes go smoothly. And the drain-service prep guide tells you how to get your home ready for our visit (and what not to pour down the drain beforehand).

Seasonal Checklists: Beat the Calendar

Plumbing problems follow the seasons, so our guides do too. Fall brings the pre-winter checklist: disconnect hoses, shut off exterior faucets, know where your main valve is before the first freeze. Winter covers frozen-pipe prevention and what to do in the first ten minutes if a pipe bursts. Spring is for sump pump testing before the rains, and summer is the ideal window for the bigger projects — repipes, sewer work, water heater replacements — while the weather cooperates.

Money Reads: Costs, Quotes & the Big Decisions

Nobody likes surprise invoices, so we wrote the guides we wish every homeowner had. Typical project costs for common Redmond jobs, demystified. Why three quotes for the same job can differ by thousands — scope, materials, and warranty terms decoded. The 50% rule for repair-versus-replace decisions, with real math. And the little-known process for getting your water utility bill adjusted after a leak, including exactly what documentation the utility wants to see.

Safety-First Reads: Gas, Permits & Boundaries

Some topics deserve extra care. Our gas line safety guide draws the line clearly: what a homeowner may touch, what only a licensed pro should touch, and the exact protocol if you smell gas. The permits guide explains which Redmond jobs need a city permit, who pulls it, and what the inspection involves — because unpermitted work becomes your problem at sale time. These are the guides to read before a project, not after.

How to Get the Most From the Library

A library is only useful if you can find things. Here is how to use ours. Searching by symptom: start with the problem-finder walkthrough, then follow its links into the deeper guides — each one stands alone but connects to the next step. Bookmark the seasonal checklists and set a phone reminder: the fall winterization guide in October, the sump pump test in March. Read the cost guides before you call any plumber, not just us — you will recognize a padded quote instantly. And share them: the leak-documentation guide has saved more than one neighbor's insurance claim, and the gas safety guide is worth sending to anyone with an older home.

We add new guides whenever we keep answering the same question on the job — the library grows out of real Redmond service calls, not a content calendar. If you have a question we have not covered, call and ask: if it is a good one, it may become the next guide, and we will send you the link when it is published. That is how several of our most-read pieces started — as a homeowner's late-evening question that deserved a proper answer. The library is free, it is always growing, and unlike most free advice on the internet, every word of it has been tested under an actual Redmond sink.

A final suggestion: read the repair-versus-replace guide before your next big plumbing decision, even if you are sure you already know the answer. It is the guide our own technicians wish every customer read first, because it reframes the question from "what is cheapest today" to "what costs least over the next ten years" — and those are very different questions with very different answers. Pair it with the quotes-decoded guide, and you will walk into any estimate appointment — ours or anyone's — as the most informed person in the room. That is the entire point of the library.
